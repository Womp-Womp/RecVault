RecVault

Continuous voice recording on Android, backed up to your own Google Drive, with a loud alarm the moment a backup is missing.

I built RecVault so my Poppy could tell me his life story, in his own voice, as he went into hospice, and so not one word of it would ever be lost.

"I use it; I wouldn't if I hadn't needed to 100% rely on it perfectly. Evidence provided when requested :)."

In plain words

  • Records all day, even with the screen off and the phone in your pocket.
  • Copies every recording to your own Google Drive within about 15 minutes of it being made.
  • Double-checks every copy. It asks Google what it actually stored and compares it to the phone, byte for byte.
  • Rings like an alarm clock if anything goes wrong: a copy goes missing, recording stops, or the phone runs low on space. The alarm says why and won't swipe away until it's fixed.
  • Never deletes anything. Your recordings stay on the phone even if you uninstall the app.
  • A dead battery costs seconds, not the recording. Everything up to the moment the phone died still plays.
  • Private. No ads, no tracking, no company servers. Your recordings go from your phone to your Drive and nowhere else.
  • Light on space: about 350 MB a day of nonstop recording.

Install

  1. Open this page on your Android phone and tap Download.
  2. Tap the downloaded file. Allow installs from that source when asked. If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, tap More details › Install anyway.
  3. Open RecVault: allow microphone, notifications and audio; tap Let RecVault run unrestricted; tap Connect Google Drive; tap Start recording.

Android 10 or newer. Any Google account can connect; RecVault asks only for access to the files it creates.

Technical details

Recording
FormatAAC-LC in an ADTS stream (.aac), mono, 32 kHz, 32 kbps, phone microphone
Chunks15 minutes each, back to back; the switch gap is measured and shown in the app
Power-loss safetyADTS frames (~32 ms each) are self-contained, so a cut-off chunk plays up to its last written frame
Storage use~14 MB/hour · ~350 MB/day · ~10.5 GB/month
Saved toRecordings/RecVault/ (Android 12+) or Music/RecVault/ (Android 10–11), public MediaStore folder that survives uninstall
Backup
DestinationMy Drive › RecVault › <phone name>, via Drive API v3 resumable upload
Google permissiondrive.file only: can create files and see only its own files
WhenRight after each chunk closes, plus a WorkManager pass every 15 minutes (Android may delay it slightly)
Confirmed whenA fresh listing of the Drive folder shows the same name, size and MD5 as the phone; upload responses alone are never trusted
RetriesAutomatic and idempotent: a crash or lost connection can cause a re-upload, never a loss
Alarms (sound + vibration, not swipeable, clear themselves when fixed)
Not backed upAny recording unconfirmed 75 minutes after it started
Recording offThe phone stopped the recorder, or Android blocked a restart (e.g. after reboot): tap to resume
Mic stuckNo audio written for 3 minutes (the recorder restarts itself after 90 seconds first)
Low storageUnder 2 GB free on the phone
Internal errorA backup pass or the alarm check itself failed
App
RequiresAndroid 10+ (API 29), Google Play services for sign-in
PermissionsMicrophone, microphone foreground service, notifications, read audio, internet, wake lock, start at boot, battery-optimization exemption request
Code it shipsKotlin, AndroidX, WorkManager, Google Play services auth. No ad, analytics or crash-reporting SDKs
Size6.7 MB APK, signed with APK Signature Scheme v2
ProofsLean 4 (core, no Mathlib): 17 theorems incl. alarm_silent_sound, stale_quiet_sound, reach_keeps_local, cache_never_empty, verified_progress
Not coveredAndroid, Google's servers, the compiler and the mic hardware can't be proven; the alarms watch for them instead
Known limitsAndroid 14+ won't let any app restart the mic on its own after a reboot or update (you get a tap-to-resume alarm). If the phone force-kills the app, it can't warn you, so set battery to Unrestricted

Proven, not promised

The backup rules are checked by 17 machine-verified theorems in Lean 4, and the app's own code is tested against each one. The headline theorem: if the alarm is quiet, every recording older than 75 minutes is confirmed in the cloud. Android, Google's servers and the microphone can't be proven, which is why the alarm exists. Details.
